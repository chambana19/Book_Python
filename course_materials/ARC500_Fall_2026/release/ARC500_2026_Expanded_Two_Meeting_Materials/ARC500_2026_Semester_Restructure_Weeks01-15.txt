================================================================

  Weekly Studio Assignments (13 graded weeks; lowest 2 dropped) ... 40%
  Project 1 — Evidence Before Design (mid-semester, due Week 8)  ... 25%
  Project 2 — Predict, Optimize, Decide (final, due Week 15)     ... 35%
                                                            TOTAL   100%

  (Both independently drafted versions of this plan arrived at this exact
  40/25/35 split without seeing each other's work — kept as strong signal
  the balance is right, and untouched by the critique/revision pass below,
  which found issues only inside the two projects' internal rubrics.)

  Policy notes (deliberately NOT weighted line items):
  - Attendance/professionalism: a flat deduction on the final composite
    score (first two absences per semester automatically excused; each
    unexcused absence after that is a small fixed deduction, e.g. 1-2
    points off the final numeric grade) — carried over from FS25 rather
    than reinvented.
  - AI-use disclosure: required inside every weekly assignment's and every
    project's own rubric (a small internal percentage there), not a fourth
    top-level line — and it also functions as a compliance gate: a
    missing, incomplete, or fabricated disclosure caps that submission's
    score at 70% regardless of technical quality.
  - No separate quiz/exam category.

  Reasoning for the three-component model (and for what was cut):

  - A zero-prior-experience cohort meeting twice weekly for 80 minutes
    benefits most from a short, frequent, recoverable feedback loop, not a
    few high-stakes tests — hence 40% earned in small weekly steps, with
    the two lowest dropped so one bad week isn't catastrophic.

  - FS25 spent 40% of the grade on two closed-book, syntax-recall quizzes.
    That model is dropped and not replaced by an equivalent test: if
    students are expected to use AI coding assistants throughout, a
    memorized-syntax exam measures the wrong thing, and the weekly
    assignments already provide the frequent, individually-attributable
    check the quizzes were providing — just distributed instead of
    concentrated into two high-stakes sittings.

  - Project 2 outweighs Project 1 (35% vs. 25%) because it is cumulative
    across the entire semester and requires genuinely integrating two
    skill families (evaluated prediction and optimization) into one
    pipeline, a harder assessment-worthy task than Project 1's four
    largely sequential skills.

2. WEEKLY ASSIGNMENT DESIGN
================================================================

  THE DECISION, STATED PLAINLY: a "weekly assignment" IS that week's
  Meeting B Spyder studio handout, completed and submitted as the graded
  artifact — not a second, separately-designed deliverable layered on top.

  Why this choice, not a separate reinforcement task: the alternative (a
  distinct 20-40 minute task on different data, graded independently) was
  seriously considered because it forces transfer rather than copy-paste.
  It was rejected as the PRIMARY structure because a zero-prior-experience
  audience meeting only 80 minutes twice a week cannot absorb a doubled
  weekly deliverable without either slowing new-content pace or making the
  take-home burden the single biggest source of weekly stress — and a solo
  instructor cannot sustainably author two original weekly artifacts for
  13 different weeks on top of two full project specs.

  THE ONE ADDITION — A LIGHTWEIGHT TRANSFER SAFEGUARD: starting Week 4,
  every week's CHECKS-INTERPRET section must include at least one
  self-generated check applied to a slightly different input than the one
  worked through live in Meeting B (a different row, case, parameter
  value, or small scenario tweak), with 2-4 assert statements the student
  writes and runs themselves before submitting. This buys most of the
  independent-transfer benefit of a second file, inside the one artifact,
  at no extra authoring cost.

  WHAT MAKES IT GRADEABLE, CONSISTENTLY, EVERY WEEK: every Meeting B
  handout uses the same four-heading "# %%" structure — QUESTION /
  INPUTS/ASSUMPTIONS / METHOD / CHECKS/INTERPRET. That structure IS the
  rubric skeleton. [CORRECTED Aug 18: this line previously read "standard

  "first handout to carry the standard four-heading cell structure." Both
  are now stale: the Week 1B and 2B handouts were subsequently written to
  the same standard. The package contains 14 instructor/student handout
