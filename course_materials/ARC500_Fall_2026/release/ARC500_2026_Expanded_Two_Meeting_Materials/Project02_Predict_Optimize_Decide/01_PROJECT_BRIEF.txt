ARC 500 — PROJECT 2 BRIEF
PREDICT, OPTIMIZE, DECIDE: PERFORMANCE-INFORMED DESIGN ALTERNATIVES

PROJECT QUESTION
How can an evaluated approximation help search a bounded design space without
being mistaken for simulation, measurement, or professional judgment?

Use Data/radley_portfolio_envelope.csv (the canonical 140-row, 9-column dataset used
in Weeks 13–15) or one instructor-approved equivalent with a written schema mapping.
Data/ARC500_facade_performance.csv is a legacy miniature example and is not the
default Project 2 model dataset.

PART I — FORMULATE, PREPARE DATA, AND TEST SENSITIVITY
- Define design variables, units, bounds, objective, constraints, and baseline.
- Choose regression for a continuous outcome OR classification for a go/no-go call.
- Audit the data and check at least one possible outlier. This outlier check is a
  required data-preparation step, not an optional one.
- Run one one- or two-variable sensitivity sweep.
- Justify the primary optimization family from the problem structure.

PART II — OPTIMIZE WITH ONE PRIMARY FAMILY
- Solve the full project with ONE primary family:
  A. linear programming, B. bounded nonlinear minimize, or C. heuristic search.
- Demonstrate scipy.optimize.linprog on the linear sub-component, or a simplified
  linear relaxation, of your own problem in the Week 10 studio, recomputing every
  constraint by hand. This demonstration is required even when your primary family
  is bounded nonlinear minimize or heuristic search.
- Report sensitivity to starting state or random seed where relevant.
- Verify all constraints for the selected candidate.

PART III — COMPARE SOLVERS ON A BOUNDED SUB-PROBLEM
- Compare the primary method with ONE transparent alternative on a simplified
  bounded sub-problem.
- Report solution quality, feasibility, and runtime.

PART IV — EVALUATE ONE PREDICTION MODEL AND INTEGRATE IT (REQUIRED)
- Create X and y with an explicit feature list and no leakage.
- Reserve the final test set before fitting. Use validation or out-of-fold evidence
  for feature, model, and classification-threshold choices; open the final test once,
  only after those choices are frozen.
- Fit a naive baseline and one proposed model.
- Regression track: MAE, RMSE, R², and residual plot.
- Classification track: confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, class/support counts,
  probability-calibration status, and a consequence-based threshold chosen without
  looking at the final test labels.
- Complete the model card and refuse unsupported extrapolation.
- Use the fitted model's prediction/probability as an objective or constraint
  feeding the Part II and/or Part III search. This integration is the project's
  central, required move. Use the Week 14 four-feature specification and evaluation
  protocol for the chosen track; freeze/export the exact fitted artifact that the
  optimizer calls so feature order and threshold cannot silently change.
- Complete an integration smoke test by the end of Week 14: implement the
  model-inside-objective/constraint wrapper; evaluate it at the baseline and one
  candidate; verify feature order plus featurewise and joint-support gates. Week 15
  is for independent confirmation, communication, and polish—not first assembly.
- Compare the baseline design and recommended candidate.
- Keep the search inside both featurewise ranges and a documented joint-support rule;
  in-range but unrepresented feature combinations must be refused or escalated.
- Verify both baseline and selected candidate with the best available non-surrogate
  evidence. A surrogate optimum is not a final recommendation until this check exists.
- State a defensible uncertainty method (RMSE alone is not a prediction interval), at
  least three limitations, and the next validation step.

DELIVERABLES
1. One-page formulation and sensitivity memo.
2. Two or three .py files.
3. Model metric table, diagnostic figure, and model card.
4. Primary optimization result, compact comparison, constraint table, solver card.
5. Four figures: sensitivity/design space; model diagnostic; solver comparison;
   baseline versus recommended design. Animation is optional.
6. A 1,000–1,200-word report.
7. Five-minute presentation: four-minute decision brief plus one-minute
   evidence/code trace; hard cap five minutes per student.
8. README and AI-use record.

WEEK 15 REVIEW FORMAT
- Reserve 15 minutes for selective source-deck synthesis after project reviews.
- If enrollment exceeds 12, use parallel gallery/code-review rounds, then reconvene
  for synthesis; do not lengthen the five-minute individual cap.

AI USE
AI may scaffold code, explanations, and debugging. It may not supply unverified
numbers, hide data leakage, choose a threshold without consequences, or turn a
model result into an unsupported decision. Record accepted, modified, and
rejected/verified suggestions.
